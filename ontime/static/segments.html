The funnel

A segment is one (route, from-stop, to-stop, hour, weekday/weekend) bucket. Seeing one is not the same as knowing it, and knowing it is not the same as being able to say so with any confidence. Each step below drops what the next one cannot use.

Measured against the timetable, not against what happened to be observed: the denominator is every segment-bucket the cached schedule implies, so this is how much of the model is still missing rather than how much of the traffic was caught.

How many samples each segment has

The mass of what has been learned, and where the gates fall across it.

The confidence interval on a segment's median is distribution-free: it is built from the order statistics of the raw samples — the k-th smallest observation and the k-th largest — so it assumes nothing about the shape of the traversal-time distribution, which is not normal and could not be treated as such (bounded below by the road, unbounded above by traffic). The freedom costs something at the bottom of the range. The widest interval those statistics can offer is the full observed spread, min to max, and even that covers the true median only with probability 1 − 2/2ⁿ. That expression first reaches 95% at n = 6. So at five samples — exactly the threshold the predictor uses — there is no 95% interval at any width: not a wide one, none. The gate sits one observation short of the smallest sample that could support the claim being made on it.
